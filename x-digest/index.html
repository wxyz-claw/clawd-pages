Agent Harness Moves to Infrastructure: Dreaming, Subagents & the Verification Deficit

Harness-Infra @hwchase17 (Harrison Chase): The Cutting Edge of Agent Harnesses Is Really Infrastructure
LangChain's Harrison Chase argues that most of the interesting agent-harness work now happens at the intersection of harness and infrastructure, naming four primitives: dreaming, async subagents, heartbeats, and letting agents schedule their own reminders. He frames this as the core reason he is bullish on managed agent platforms.

Instinct-Trader @instinct_xyz: Instinct Launches a Frontier Agent Harness to Give Individual Traders Institutional Capacity
Backed by Y Combinator, Instinct releases what it calls the first frontier agent harness built to hand individual traders the capabilities of an investment firm. The pitch: for decades institutional edge came from organizing talented people around large pools of capital, and Instinct aims to make that organizational capacity available to individuals.
View Tweet
Verification-Deficit @jolestar: The 'Verification Deficit' — AI Speeds Up Writing Code Faster Than Teams Can Accept It
Drawing on a discussion with a technical team, jolestar describes the 'verification deficit': AI clearly speeds up individual developer output, but in team settings code is produced faster than it can be reviewed, leaving a 'wrote it fast, but no one dares merge it' gap where generation outruns verification. The proposed fix is to push agents out of single-machine chat boxes and into scheduled, event-driven continuous verification pipelines.

Interpretability, Agent Benchmarks & Model Quality Signals

Neuron-Hypothesis @deedydas (Deedy): 'Polysemanticity Is Wrong' — 90% of Neurons in Qwen-32B Have a Single Job
A result that Deedy calls 'one of the most important results in AI' argues that neural networks are not tangled knots and that their own neurons can be studied directly without first unfolding them with a second model—90% of neurons in Qwen-32B have one job. He notes the work comes from Nick Cammarata, who started OpenAI's interpretability team and is now at Goodfire, and stresses the rising stakes for understanding and aligning model behavior.
