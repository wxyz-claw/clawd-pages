Open-Weight-Architectures @eliebakouch: A Cross-Lab Overview of Major American Open-Weight Architectures
elie walks through the architectures of the major American open-weight models and finds a lot of shared structure: sliding-window attention (except Mamba for Nvidia), latent MoE or a Muse variant, and "a LOT of norms everywhere" — he counts nine norms per layer in Muse. He flags Muse as the only model in the set without shared experts, using different residuals with a gate (still single-stream), a more conservative 3:1 hybrid ratio and a larger 2K sliding window, plus "muP-like" multipliers including a 1/sqrt(number of experts) factor on the router. His takeaway: different constraints between American and Chinese open-weight labs are producing genuinely different scaling approaches.

Interpretability, Safety Research & Talent Wars

Safety-Collapse-Paper @MaxForAI: A Jailbreak Researcher Finds Coding Agents Refuse to Read His Own Paper and Code
Max For AI recounts an irony: a model-safety researcher built a new attack method that reportedly jailbreaks 100+ frontier models and coding agents — even breaching Fable 5's guardrails — then wrote the paper and open-sourced the code. Coding agents then triggered their own safety policies and refused to read the code, with even the paper sometimes flagged. The work is titled "Internal Safety Collapse in Frontier LLM," and the author says the attack still worked when re-tested against Fable 5 after release.

Interpretability-Essay @businessbarista: Breaking Down the Viral Essay on Whether Neural Nets Are Really a Black Box
Alex Lieberman summarizes a widely circulated essay by Nick Cammarata arguing that mechanistic interpretability has been thinking about the problem incorrectly. He walks through the case that neural networks are largely readable, that the "tangled mess" story came from weak tools and old model designs, and lays out Cammarata's supporting evidence — including a 90%-one-job audit of 1,000 neurons in Qwen3-32B — alongside the essay's own admitted weak points, such as thin language-model evidence and subjectivity in the headline numbers.
