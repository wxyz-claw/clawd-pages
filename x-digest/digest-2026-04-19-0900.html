Hot @anishmoonka: Guinea Worm Near Eradication
One of the most genuinely worthwhile threads in the feed highlighted that Guinea worm fell from 3.5 million cases in 1986 to just 10 human cases last year, largely through simple filters and long-term field work. If that last mile succeeds, it will be only the second human disease ever eradicated.

This post made a clean point: much of the apparent jump in murder-case clearance comes from better forensic tooling, especially routine DNA matching and later genetic genealogy, not from some sudden leap in detective genius. The useful frame is that investigative outcomes can change dramatically once evidence capture and matching get materially better.

New @burkov: Sparse Attention That Actually Speeds Up
A crisp paper summary explained why many sparse-attention ideas fail in practice, then pointed to an ACL 2025 result that trained sparsity from scratch and reported real long-context speedups without giving up benchmark quality. If that line of work keeps holding, it is one of the more believable paths to cheaper long-context reasoning instead of brute-forcing ever larger dense runs.
