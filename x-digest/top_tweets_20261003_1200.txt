Text: Most AI filmmakers spend hours improving prompts, models, and visual quality.  But a perfectly generated shot can still look amateur. Usually, the problem is composition.  1. Composition is not decoration Good composition controls: → where the viewer looks first → how important the subject feels → how much space surrounds them → how deep or flat the image feels → how characters relate to each other → where movement is allowed to happen The goal is not to make every frame “beautiful.” The goal is to make the frame intentional.  2. Learn these composition techniques first → Rule of Thirds: Place the subject away from center, usually near a third line. Useful when the environment matters or the character needs space to look or move into. → Center Composition: Put the subject directly on the center axis. Strong for introductions, confrontation, formality, power, or visual simplicity. → Negative Space: Keep a large area intentionally empty. Useful for isolation, scale, vulnerability, anticipation, or suggesting something outside the frame. → Leading Lines: Use roads, walls, railings, shadows, or architecture to guide the eye toward the important subject. → Symmetry: Balance the major visual elements around a central axis. Useful for order, precision, controlled environments, or deliberate artificiality. → Frame Within a Frame: Use a doorway, window, arch, mirror, or foreground structure to surround the subject. Great for isolation, observation, secrecy, or depth. → Foreground–Midground–Background: Build multiple readable spatial layers instead of placing a character against a flat background. One of the easiest ways to make AI images feel more cinematic. → Visual Balance: A frame does not need to be symmetrical to feel balanced. Better: “Medium-wide shot of a lone man positioned on the left third of the frame, eyes near the upper-third line, large negative space extending in the direction of his gaze, blurred foreground architecture creating depth, distant city softly separated in the background.”  Now the model knows: → where the subject goes → where the eyes sit → where the empty space belongs → which direction the character faces → how the foreground and background should work  4.
